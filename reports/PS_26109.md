# SIH 2026 Problem Statement Report

## 1. Problem Statement Overview

- **Problem ID:** 26109
- **Problem Title:** AI-enabled Predictive Forecasting System for Bovine Mastitis
- **Organization:** Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying (Implied)
- **Category:** Hardware / Software
- **Theme:** Agriculture / Animal Husbandry
- **Difficulty Level:** Medium / Hard

### Problem Statement

> Bovine mastitis is a prevalent and economically significant disease affecting dairy cattle. It impacts milk production and animal health, and is usually detected only after clinical signs appear. The increasing adoption of digital dairy technologies presents an opportunity to leverage AI, ML, and IoT for early disease prediction. The objective is to develop a predictive forecasting system to identify the risk of mastitis 7-14 days before clinical signs, using farm data and IoT sensors, providing alerts to farmers and veterinarians.

### One-Line Summary

An IoT wearable and AI dashboard system to monitor dairy cows and predict udder infections (mastitis) weeks before they become visibly sick.

---

## 2. Problem Understanding

### What is the actual problem?

Cows get an infection called mastitis, which ruins their milk and makes them sick. Currently, farmers only notice it when the cow's udder is already swollen or the milk looks bad. By then, the cow needs antibiotics and her milk must be thrown away. 

### Who faces this problem?

Dairy farmers, dairy cooperatives (like Amul), and veterinarians.

### Why is this problem important?

Mastitis causes massive economic losses to farmers due to discarded milk and vet bills. It also leads to antibiotic overuse in cattle (which causes AMR).

### Existing Problems / Limitations

Current detection relies on physical symptoms or testing the milk physically (Somatic Cell Count) when the infection is already present. There is no proactive early warning system.

---

## 3. Expected Solution

### What is SIH asking us to build?

An end-to-end IoT and AI system. Hardware sensors (collars or milk sensors) collect temperature and activity data. An AI model analyzes this data (along with farm history) to give the cow a risk score, warning the farmer 7-14 days before the cow actually gets visibly sick.

### Proposed Solution Concept

A smart collar (IoT wearable) that monitors the cow's body temperature, rumination (chewing) activity, and general movement. The data goes to a mobile app via Wi-Fi/LoRa. An ML model detects slight abnormalities in activity and temperature trends (early signs of infection) and sends an SMS/App alert to the farmer.

### Key Features

- Smart IoT collar/wearable for cattle.
- Edge or Cloud-based AI model to predict disease probability.
- Multilingual mobile app for farmers.
- Dashboard for veterinarians to monitor herd health.

## 6. Data Requirements

### Required Data

Time-series data of cow temperature, activity, milk yield, and mastitis occurrence history.

### Data Availability

**Level:** Moderate

### Possible Data Sources

- Public datasets on cattle behavior and mastitis (e.g., from research papers or Kaggle).
- Synthesizing data based on veterinary literature (e.g., "activity drops by 20% and temp rises by 0.5C a week before clinical mastitis").

### Data Challenges

Getting real, high-quality historical sensor data mapped to actual mastitis events will be difficult. We will likely need to rely on synthetic data based on real-world veterinary parameters.

## 8. MVP Feasibility for SIH

### Can we build an MVP during the hackathon?

**Verdict:** Yes

### MVP Scope

A physical "Smart Cow Collar" using an ESP32, battery, accelerometer, and temp sensor. Put it on a stuffed toy cow. A mobile app showing the "Cow Profile". Manually heat the temp sensor and hold the toy still (simulating fever/lethargy). The app immediately shows the Mastitis Risk Score jumping from "Low" to "High Risk (Prediction 9 days out)" and sends an SMS.

### What should NOT be attempted during the hackathon?

- Automated milking machine sensors (too complex physically).
- GPS tracking over large areas (focus purely on the health parameters).

### Estimated Development Difficulty

**Level:** Medium

## 12. Risks and Challenges

| Risk / Challenge | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Questionable AI accuracy | High | Heavily research veterinary papers to ensure our synthetic data rules (e.g. temp thresholds) are scientifically accurate. |

### Biggest Technical Risk
