# SIH 2026 Problem Statement Report

## 1. Problem Statement Overview

- **Problem ID:** 26144
- **Problem Title:** High-Sensitivity Atmospheric Microbarometer Infrasound Sensor
- **Organization:** DRDO / ISRO / Earth Sciences (Implied)
- **Category:** Hardware
- **Theme:** Sensors / Earth Sciences / Defence
- **Difficulty Level:** Very Hard

### Problem Statement

> Infrasound sensors detect low-frequency atmospheric pressure waves (<20 Hz) caused by explosions, volcanoes, and rocket launches. The requirement is to design and develop a high-sensitivity atmospheric microbarometer addressing the complete hardware architecture (transducer, analog front-end, temperature compensation, long-period equalization, and wind-noise reduction). 

### One-Line Summary

Design and build a highly sensitive, lab-grade analog sensor from scratch to detect incredibly faint, low-frequency sound waves in the atmosphere.

---

## 2. Problem Understanding

### What is the actual problem?

Standard microphones cannot pick up "infrasound" (sounds below 20Hz) over long distances. Commercial sensors for this are extremely expensive or restricted. The government wants an indigenously developed, highly precise hardware sensor built from the component level.

### Who faces this problem?

Meteorological departments, defence (monitoring distant explosions), and geophysical researchers.

### Why is this problem important?

Crucial for national security (detecting covert explosions) and early disaster warning (volcanoes, severe weather).

### Existing Problems / Limitations

High cost of imported sensors; lack of indigenous deep-tech hardware manufacturing for microbarometers.

---

## 3. Expected Solution

### What is SIH asking us to build?

A physical, functional hardware sensor. We must design the physical pressure transducer, the ultra-low-noise analog amplification circuitry, the physical enclosure, and the acoustic filters (wind-noise reduction). It must be tested in a lab for sensitivity and frequency response.

### Proposed Solution Concept

Using a differential capacitive or piezoresistive pressure sensor as the base element, building a custom PCB with ultra-low noise instrumentation amplifiers (like AD620 or better), adding thermal compensation circuits, and housing it in a precisely machined casing with porous hoses to cancel wind noise.

### Key Features

- Frequency detection: 0.01 Hz to 20 Hz.
- Low-noise analog front-end.
- Mechanical wind-noise reduction.
- Lab-tested sensitivity and noise floor.
