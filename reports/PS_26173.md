# SIH 2026 Problem Statement Report

## 1. Problem Statement Overview

- **Problem ID:** 26173
- **Problem Title:** Offline STT and TTS Android App for 10 Indian Languages
- **Organization:** Defence / Disaster Management (Implied)
- **Category:** Software
- **Theme:** Disaster Management / Communication
- **Difficulty Level:** Hard

### Problem Statement

> In distress scenarios, transmitting audio over low data-rate links is difficult. An Android App is required that runs highly accurate STT (Speech-to-Text) and TTS (Text-to-Speech) locally (offline) for 10 Indian languages. It should detect speech, convert to text, stream the text via Wi-Fi/Bluetooth to another phone, and that phone will convert the text back to loud speech (Walkie-Talkie style). No internet APIs allowed.

### One-Line Summary

An offline walkie-talkie Android app that converts your voice to text, sends the text over Bluetooth, and reads it out loud on the receiver's phone in 10 Indian languages.

---

## 2. Problem Understanding

### What is the actual problem?

During disasters (floods, earthquakes) or in remote military zones, internet and high-speed data are completely down. You cannot send voice notes. However, you *can* send tiny text messages over Bluetooth or low-bandwidth radio. But illiterate users or people in a panic need voice. The app compresses voice to text, sends the text, and turns it back to voice.

### Who faces this problem?

Disaster response teams, military personnel, and civilians in disaster zones.

### Why is this problem important?

Saves lives when communication infrastructure fails. Inclusivity for non-literate people.

### Existing Problems / Limitations

Google Translate / Google Voice works great, but requires an active internet connection. Fully offline, highly accurate models for 10 regional Indian languages don't easily fit on a low-end smartphone.

---

## 3. Expected Solution

### What is SIH asking us to build?

An Android application acting like a Push-to-Talk walkie-talkie. It must:
1. Work 100% offline.
2. Support 10 languages (Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, etc.).
3. Have embedded TinyML STT and TTS models.
4. Establish a local Wi-Fi Direct or Bluetooth connection between two phones.
5. Transmit the transcribed text instantly.
6. Play the received text as an un-interruptible loud alert.

### Proposed Solution Concept

An Android app built in Kotlin/Java. It uses Vosk-API or TensorFlow Lite for offline Speech-to-Text. When the user stops talking, the text is sent via Android Nearby Connections API (Bluetooth/Wi-Fi Direct) to a paired phone. The receiving phone uses an offline TTS engine (like Android's built-in TextToSpeech, if offline data is downloaded, or a custom lightweight model) to play the audio.

### Key Features

- Fully offline inference.
- Push-to-Talk (PTT) interface.
- Automatic silence detection (VAD - Voice Activity Detection).
- Multi-lingual support.
- Bluetooth / Local Wi-Fi networking.

### Data Availability

**Level:** Moderate

### Possible Data Sources

- AI4Bharat (IndicWav2Vec, IndicTTS).

### Data Challenges

The main challenge is *size*. A good offline TTS/STT model for ONE language is often 50-100MB. Fitting 10 languages into an app while satisfying the "efficiency/low RAM" metric will be extremely difficult.

## 12. Risks and Challenges

| Risk / Challenge | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Models are too large | High | Use extreme quantization (INT8) or rely on Android's built-in offline language packs for TTS. |
| High Latency | Medium | Stream the text word-by-word over Bluetooth instead of waiting for the full sentence. |

### Biggest Technical Risk

The AI models take 10 seconds to process the speech on a low-end phone, violating the "low latency / real-time factor" requirement.

### Biggest Project Risk

Failing to connect the two phones reliably via Android APIs, making the demo impossible.

## 15. Final Verdict

### Recommendation

**CONSIDER**

### Why?

This is a very strong, high-impact software problem. If the team has exceptional Android developers, this is a great choice. However, it completely sidelines the hardware members (unless you introduce custom LoRa communicators). Furthermore, compressing 10 language models to work offline on a cheap phone is mathematically and computationally very frustrating for a 48-hour hackathon.

### Biggest Advantage

Offline mobile AI is highly impressive and has a clear, understandable demo.

### Biggest Disadvantage

Zero hardware work, and mobile ML integration can be extremely buggy.

### Final Question

> **If we had to build the MVP tomorrow, could our current team realistically build and demonstrate it?**

**Answer:** Maybe

**Reason:** We could easily build the Bluetooth chat app, but integrating fully offline STT/TTS for multiple Indian languages using open-source models within Android (JNI/C++) takes significant trial and error.
